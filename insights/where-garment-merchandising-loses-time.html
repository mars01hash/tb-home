From tech pack to costing sheet: where garment merchandising loses time

We timed a merchandising team for a month. Most of the hours went on retyping and chasing, not decisions.

TechBeez RMG solutions team 16 April 2024 5 min read

On this page

  1. What we found
  2. Why it matters
  3. What to fix first
  4. Key takeaways

Merchandisers are the hub of a garment export business. They talk to buyers, mills, trim suppliers, the sample room, planning and commercial. So where does their week actually go?

We shadowed a merchandising team at an export factory for a month and logged where the hours went, from the moment a tech pack arrived to the moment a costing was sent to the buyer.

What we found

Most time was not spent on the decisions merchandisers are hired for, such as choosing fabric, negotiating prices or solving fit problems. It went on moving information from one place to another:

  • Retyping the tech pack. Styles, BOM lines, colourways and size breakdowns were copied by hand from PDFs into Excel, then again into booking sheets.
  • Collecting consumption. Fabric consumption came from CAD in one file, and labour costs from the IE team in another. Both were pasted into the costing sheet manually.
  • Chasing rates. Trim and fabric prices were looked up in old emails and previous costings, because there was no shared price library.
  • Re-checking. Because everything was copied, everything had to be checked again, often by a senior merchandiser late in the evening.

Why it matters

Slow costing is not just an internal problem. Buyers compare response times across suppliers, and a quote that arrives two days later can lose the order. Copying errors are worse: a wrong consumption figure or a missed trim can turn a profitable order into a loss that only shows up at order closing.

The pattern behind itEvery manual copy is both a delay and a chance to introduce an error. Removing copies saves time twice: once in typing and once in checking.

What to fix first

  1. One source for each piece of data. Styles and BOMs entered once, then reused by costing, booking and purchasing.
  2. Import, do not retype. Bring CAD consumption and IE costing files in directly, in the format those teams already produce.
  3. A shared fabric and trim library. Current rates by supplier, so costing starts from known prices.
  4. Costing templates by product type. So a new quote starts from a sensible structure rather than an old file.

None of this needs AI to start. It needs data to be entered once and trusted. That foundation is also what makes automation possible later.

Key takeaways

  • Most merchandising time goes on moving and checking data, not deciding.
  • Slow or wrong costings cost orders and margin.
  • Enter each piece of data once, import CAD and IE files, and keep a shared price library.
