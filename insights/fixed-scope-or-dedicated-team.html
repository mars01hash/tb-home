Fixed scope or dedicated team: how to choose

A simple way to decide which engagement fits your stage, budget and roadmap.

TechBeez Solutions team 18 November 2026 4 min read

On this page

  1. The two models in one paragraph each
  2. Side by side
  3. Choose fixed scope when
  4. Choose a dedicated team when
  5. The common path: start fixed, then switch
  6. Key takeaways

Clients often ask which engagement model is cheaper. The honest answer is that the cheaper one is the one that fits how much you already know about what you are building.

The two models in one paragraph each

Fixed scope means we agree a defined set of features, a timeline and a price up front. You know the cost before you commit, and changes go through a simple change request.

Dedicated team means you get a stable team of designers and engineers for a monthly fee, working through a backlog you prioritise every week. The scope can move as fast as you learn.

Side by side

Fixed scopeDedicated team
Best whenThe requirements are clear and unlikely to changeYou are still learning what users need
BudgetKnown up frontPredictable per month, total depends on duration
ChangesThrough change requestsRe-prioritised every week
Typical length4 to 16 weeks3 months or longer
Your time neededMostly at the start and at reviewsA product owner every week

Choose fixed scope when

  • You are building a well-defined first version, such as an MVP to test with early users or investors.
  • You have a hard budget ceiling that cannot move.
  • The project replaces an existing system whose behaviour is already understood.

Choose a dedicated team when

  • The product is live and the roadmap changes with every round of user feedback.
  • You need to grow an in-house team quickly, without months of hiring.
  • The work is ongoing: new features, integrations, performance and support.

The common path: start fixed, then switch

Many of our clients use both. A fixed-scope first release proves the idea and the working relationship with a known budget. Once the product is live and the backlog starts to move every week, the same people continue as a dedicated team, so nothing is lost in a handover.

Not sure yet?Bring what you have to a scoping call. We will tell you which model fits, and give you a written estimate with a cost range either way.

Key takeaways

  • Fixed scope fits clear requirements and fixed budgets.
  • A dedicated team fits a live product with a moving roadmap.
  • Starting fixed and switching to a dedicated team keeps the same people and context.
